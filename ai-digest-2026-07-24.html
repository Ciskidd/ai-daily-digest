Nvidia and Microsoft lead 25-company letter urging Washington not to restrict open-weight AI

Bloomberg · Fortune · CNBC · TechCrunch — Jul 24, 2026 PolicyChina angle

A coalition of 25 US technology firms — Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, Andreessen Horowitz, IBM, Dell, Palantir, Mistral, Hugging Face and Y Combinator among them — published a joint open letter on July 24 arguing against "premature restrictions" on open-weight models, calling downloadable, inspectable weights essential to US technological leadership. The letter lands as the White House weighs a response to allegations that Moonshot distilled Anthropic's Fable model to train Kimi K3. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang amplified it with the first post he has ever made on X.

Why it matters: Open-weight policy is now the fault line of US AI regulation, and industry is mobilizing to keep it legal before any China-driven clampdown hardens.

Qualcomm angle Tailwind
Second-order but real: open, downloadable models are the fuel for on-device and edge inference — Qualcomm's core structural bet. A world of private-infra open weights expands the Snapdragon/NPU TAM versus a cloud-API-only regime. Worth watching that Qualcomm was not a named signatory.
Investor angle Mixed
Bullish for the open-model diffusion narrative (Nvidia, Hugging Face, Mistral) and edge silicon; regulatory overhang remains if Washington leans the other way. This is a lobbying/positioning signal, not new revenue — mostly not yet priced because the policy outcome is unresolved.

Stripe in talks to buy AI model marketplace OpenRouter for ~$10B

Wall Street Journal · Reuters · Yahoo Finance — Jul 24, 2026 🔒 WSJM&A

Stripe is in advanced talks to acquire OpenRouter — the marketplace that routes developer requests across 400+ models from ~70 providers to the cheapest or best option — in a deal that could value it near $10B, up from a $1.3B round in May. A final agreement could come soon, though talks remain fluid and other suitors have reportedly circled.

Why it matters: Stripe is buying into the routing-and-billing chokepoint of the AI stack, positioning for agent-driven commerce where payments and model selection converge.

Qualcomm angle Neutral
Minimal direct read — model-routing and cloud billing sit well above the silicon layer. No handset, PC, auto, or edge implication.
Investor angle Mixed
Validates AI-infra M&A froth and Stripe's aggressive push up the stack, but ~$10B for a company marked at $1.3B in May reads as late-cycle pricing. Signals demand for aggregation/routing margin; concentration and valuation risk cut the other way.

Alphabet lifts 2026 capex to $205B as free cash flow turns negative

CNBC · Bloomberg · Seeking Alpha — Jul 22–24, 2026 Follow-upEarnings

What changed since yesterday's selloff item: Alphabet's Q2 print confirmed the numbers behind the mega-cap wobble. Revenue came in at $119.8B (+24% YoY, ahead of ~$117B consensus) and cloud grew 82%, but management raised full-year 2026 capex guidance to $195–205B (from $180–190B), Q2 capex hit $44.9B (+107% YoY), and free cash flow turned negative. Shares fell ~5%.

Why it matters: It sets the bar for Microsoft/Meta (Jul 29) and Apple/Amazon (Jul 30): investors now want monetization proof, not just bigger spend.

Qualcomm angle Watch
Hyperscaler capex flows overwhelmingly to training clusters (Nvidia, Broadcom, TPUs), with little direct Qualcomm content. Second-order: a relentless cloud-inference buildout is the competitive backdrop against which Qualcomm pitches on-device inference as the cheaper alternative.
Investor angle Bearish (near-term)
Demand is clearly real (cloud +82%), but negative FCF and 107% capex growth spook the market; the spend accrues to chips, memory, and power/cooling while app-layer monetization must catch up. Partly priced after the ~5% drop, but the debate isn't settled.

Samsung and SK Hynix set to unveil large US memory supply deals during Korean president's SF visit

Crypto Briefing · DIGITIMES · DCD — Jul 24, 2026 Memory

Samsung and SK Hynix are preparing to announce sizable long-term memory supply contracts with US tech companies on July 24, timed to President Lee Jae Myung's San Francisco visit; a senior presidential official described the values as "very large and meaningful." SK Hynix leads HBM at 50–55% share, with Samsung at 35–40% and Micron trailing — against a backdrop of DRAM/HBM prices climbing sharply through 2026.

Why it matters: Multi-year lock-ins lengthen demand visibility for the memory duopoly and further tighten an already stressed AI-memory market.

Qualcomm angle Headwind
Qualcomm's data-center HBC/Dragonfly roadmap and its handset/PC BOMs both lean on LPDDR/DRAM. Tightening supply and long-term hyperscaler lock-ins raise Qualcomm's memory costs and risk crowding out allocation. Partly offset long-term: the whole HBC pitch is to cut reliance on scarce, expensive HBM.
Investor angle Bullish (memory)
Confirms the HBM/DRAM supercycle and pricing power for SK Hynix, Samsung and Micron — a demand signal, not just spend. Watch for eventual capacity glut and customer-concentration risk as the up-cycle matures.
