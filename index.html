Ninth Circuit clears Perplexity's Comet shopping agent to operate on Amazon

Courthouse News / Ninth Circuit·Aug 4

The Ninth Circuit overturned a lower-court order that had barred Perplexity's Comet shopping agent from Amazon.com, holding that it is users — not Perplexity — who 'accessed' Amazon under the federal computer-fraud statute. The ruling narrows how the CFAA applies to consumer AI agents acting on a user's behalf. Amazon had sought to block agentic access to its marketplace.

Why it matters A precedent that agents act as the user, not the vendor, removes a major legal barrier to autonomous shopping and browsing agents.

Qualcomm angleNeutral

No direct silicon linkage, though a legal green light for consumer agents supports the broader agentic-usage curve that ultimately drives more inference — a faint long-run positive for on-device and edge compute.

Investor angleMixed

Rust project publishes LLM policy: models may refine, not create

Inside Rust Blog·Aug 5

Five Rust teams adopted a policy permitting LLMs to 'answer questions, analyze, distill, refine, check, suggest, review' contributions but not to 'create' them, and it bans LLM-generated documentation and non-trivial comments. LLM reviews are advisory only, with human review still required. The move responds to a deluge of low-effort 'slop' PRs and is one of the more explicit stances a major open-source project has taken on AI-generated code.

Why it matters How flagship open-source projects govern AI-written code will shape norms for the whole ecosystem.

Qualcomm angleNeutral
