Moonshot AI to release Kimi K3 open weights tomorrow — the largest open-weight model ever

VentureBeat·drop scheduled Sun Jul 27, 00:00 UTC China Watch 译 also covered by 量子位 (QbitAI) Top: the weekend's only frontier-scale event

Beijing-based Moonshot AI will publish the full weights of Kimi K3 — a 2.8-trillion-parameter sparse mixture-of-experts model — on Hugging Face on July 27 under a Modified MIT license, making it the largest open-weight model ever released (roughly 1.4 TB in MXFP4 quantization, about 2.8× the size of K2). K3 currently tops the Frontend Code Arena at 1,679 points, ahead of GPT-5.6 Sol and Zhipu's GLM-5.2, and Moonshot's self-reported benchmarks place it above Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 while trailing Claude Fable 5. Independent testers flagged a roughly 51% hallucination rate that Moonshot left off its published charts, and the company recommends 64-plus accelerators for production self-hosting rather than a lightweight setup.

中国「月之暗面」Kimi K3 明日开放权重：2.8 万亿参数，史上最大开源模型

Why it matters: A frontier-adjacent model anyone can download and self-host collapses the price umbrella under Western closed APIs and gives enterprises a route around China-API data-residency concerns — while the omitted hallucination rate is a reminder that "open" is not the same as "reliable."

Qualcomm angle
Watch · second-order

Cheaper, self-hostable frontier weights reinforce the on-device/edge-inference thesis Qualcomm's whole AI strategy rests on — but a 1.4 TB, 64-accelerator model is a datacenter artifact. The read-through to Snapdragon NPUs runs only through distilled derivatives, so it's directionally supportive of the "inference migrates to the edge" narrative, not a direct handset or PC-silicon catalyst.

Investor angle
Mixed

Bearish for closed-model pricing power (OpenAI, Anthropic) as open weights compress the value of a raw frontier model; bullish for the inference layer (NVIDIA, HBM/DRAM, neoclouds) since serving a 1.4 TB model is compute- and memory-hungry. The "largest-ever, self-hostable" milestone is incrementally new rather than a regime change.

Kimi K3's imminent weights drop reignites the US distillation-and-sanctions fight

The Hill·Jul 25–26 Follow-up China Watch 译 Translated from Chinese · 虎嗅 (Huxiu)

What changed since we last covered this (Jul 23): with K3's open weights now hours away, the fight has escalated on three fronts. The open-weights coalition letter to policymakers has grown to roughly 35 signatories; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent repeated that US model "watermarks" are turning up in Chinese systems and floated sanctions and entity-list action; and OSTP's Michael Kratsios alleged large-scale distillation plus possible export-control breaches tied to Moonshot's use of NVIDIA GB300 servers. Chinese outlet 虎嗅 (Huxiu) reframed the whole debate as Washington wanting open weights "but with an American passport."

35 家公司联名：美国既想要开放权重，又想给「开放」加上国籍

Why it matters: The open-weights argument has shifted from safety to national origin — a framing that could pull open-source AI itself into the export-control regime.

Qualcomm angle
Headwind · second-order

Any escalation of US–China AI/semiconductor export controls and entity-list actions raises retaliation risk and a more bifurcated market. Qualcomm's China handset and IoT revenue and its Chinese-OEM Snapdragon mix sit squarely in the blast radius. Speculative for now — this is rhetoric, not rules — but directionally negative if enforcement follows.

Investor angle
Mixed

Headline risk for China-exposed AI names and for NVIDIA (fresh scrutiny of GB300 exports); a potential relative positive for US closed-model labs if enforcement blunts cheap Chinese open weights. With no actual sanctions yet, the market is pricing this as tail risk rather than fact.
