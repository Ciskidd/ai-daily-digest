Astera Labs posts record Q2, says Scorpio switch line ramps a quarter early

#1 · Top 3 Astera Labs / Yahoo Finance · Q2 FY26 · Aug 4–7

Why it made the Top 3: the cleanest hard-revenue read yet on rack-scale interconnect demand — the "picks and shovels" of every AI cluster.

Astera Labs reported record Q2 revenue of $392.4M, up 27% sequentially and 104% year-over-year, with non-GAAP EPS of $0.80 beating the $0.69 consensus. PCIe 6.0 products now exceed half of revenue (up from roughly a third in Q1), and management said its Scorpio fabric switches will become the company's largest product family in Q3 — one quarter ahead of prior guidance — with 10+ customers engaged. Q3 guidance of $540–560M and EPS of $1.16–1.21 came in well above the Street.

Why it matters: Interconnect and fabric — not just GPUs — is where scale-up dollars are landing, and Astera's guide says that demand is still accelerating into the back half.
Qualcomm angle
Watch · 2nd-order
Astera plays in data-center scale-up connectivity (PCIe/CXL/Scorpio), not a Qualcomm market. The read-through is directional: it validates the cloud-inference cluster buildout that competes with Qualcomm's on-device/edge-inference thesis, and confirms interconnect is a first-class beneficiary of AI capex. Speculative for QCOM specifically.

+104% YoY with a raised mix of PCIe 6.0 and Scorpio pulled forward is demand, not just spend, in the connectivity layer — positive read-through to Broadcom, Marvell and Credo. Caveat: ALAB is richly valued, so much of the trajectory is already priced.Not investment advice.

Memory makers race to break the AI bandwidth wall — zHBM, HBF and CXL-PNM debut

#2 · Top 3 Samsung / SK hynix / SanDisk-Kioxia · via Distill, FMS 2026 · Aug 7

Why it made the Top 3: memory is the binding constraint on AI economics; architectural shifts here ripple through the entire stack.

Samsung unveiled next-generation "zHBM," claiming 8× the performance of HBM5, alongside a "mixed-use complex semiconductor" that stacks HBM directly on the GPU. SK hynix introduced CMM-Ax, a CXL-PNM near-memory-compute solution co-developed with Marvell, and a first high-bandwidth flash (HBF) standard with SanDisk. SanDisk and Kioxia separately unveiled the industry's highest-density 10th-generation QLC NAND. All target the same problem: feeding data-hungry AI accelerators without drowning in power and cost, even as Samsung warns the memory shortage worsens into 2027.

Why it matters: The AI bottleneck has migrated from FLOPs to bytes-per-second and dollars-per-gigabyte — whoever solves memory bandwidth and capacity captures outsized value.
Qualcomm angle
Headwind · near-term
A worsening memory shortage plus premium next-gen stacks keeps DRAM/NAND prices elevated — Qualcomm already flagged memory-driven margin and guidance pressure, and richer on-device-AI phones need more LPDDR, squeezing handset BoM. Longer term, cheap high-capacity HBF/QLC could aid on-device model storage, but the near-term signal is cost pressure.
Investor angle
Bullish memory · Mixed buyers
Pricing power for Samsung, SK hynix and Micron intensifies when the constraint is capacity, not demand — bearish for memory-heavy device OEMs and system-builder margins. Structural and multi-year; with the shortage extending into 2027 it may not be fully priced.Not investment advice.

AMD acquires AI-inference-chip startup Taalas to push deeper into Nvidia's turf

#3 · Top 3 AMD · Aug 6–7

Why it made the Top 3: strategic M&A in the hottest sub-segment — custom inference silicon — signals where margin is migrating.

AMD agreed to acquire Taalas, a Toronto startup building hardwired, model-specific inference chips. Retail and trade commentary framed the deal as a move to "compete more directly with Nvidia" beyond GPUs, targeting the efficiency curve of large-scale inference rather than training. Terms were not disclosed; the deal lands the same week AMD reported record Q2 data-center revenue up 107% year-over-year.

Why it matters: As inference volume dwarfs training, purpose-built ASICs that cut cost-per-token become the battleground — and AMD is buying its way in.
Qualcomm angle
Watch
Hardwired inference silicon is exactly the on-device-vs-cloud efficiency fight Qualcomm is betting on. AMD signaling that inference cost is the prize is a competitive read for Qualcomm's two data-center custom-silicon engagements and its NPU-led edge story — mildly cautionary if it accelerates cloud-inference cost curves.
Investor angle
Mixed
A small tuck-in, not financially material near-term, but strategically AMD staking a claim on inference share versus Nvidia. Watch for a consolidation wave among inference-ASIC startups (Groq, Etched, d-Matrix). A roadmap signal more than an earnings driver.Not investment advice.

Packaging and legacy-foundry earnings hit records as AI capex lifts the whole chain

Amkor / GlobalFoundries · Q2 FY26 · late Jul–Aug

Amkor reported record Q2 2026 revenue of about $1.9 billion, and GlobalFoundries posted record second-quarter results — evidence that the AI build-out is lifting back-end packaging and legacy-node foundries, not just leading-edge logic.
