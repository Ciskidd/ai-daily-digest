Kimi K3 open weights land a day early — the largest open model ever released

Moonshot AI published full open weights for Kimi K3 on the evening of July 26, a day ahead of its July 27 target. At 2.8 trillion sparse-MoE parameters it is the largest open-weight model ever released; the weights run roughly 1.4TB in MXFP4 four-bit precision, and Together AI and Modal shipped day-0 hosted access. The model carries a 1M-token context window and native multimodal input.

Kimi K3 开源权重提前一天发布，成为史上最大开源模型（2.8 万亿参数）
Why it matters: It pushes the open-weight frontier to a scale only closed labs previously operated at, and hands self-hosters a Chinese frontier model that sidesteps API data-residency concerns.
Qualcomm angle Watch
The 1.4TB memory footprint underscores that true frontier inference stays in the datacenter, not on a phone — a structural headwind to Qualcomm's high-end on-device thesis. Second-order tailwind: giant open models fuel demand for distilled/quantized small models that do run on Snapdragon NPUs. Watch whether Moonshot ships smaller K3 variants.
Investor angle Mixed
Bearish for closed-model pricing power — free 2.8T weights compress the value of proprietary frontier APIs — but bullish for the inference/hosting and HBM/memory layer that has to serve 1.4TB-class models. Reinforces China's open-weight lead as a strategic wedge; trajectory known, but the scale is new information. Not investment advice.

Investor angle Bullish

Qualcomm's data-center ramp in focus ahead of fiscal Q3

Qualcomm's push into data-center CPUs — with Meta as an anchor customer and initial custom-silicon shipments to a leading hyperscaler guided for the December quarter — is under the spotlight as it approaches its fiscal Q3 report. Management targets $15B data-center sales and $40B non-handset revenue by FY2029, aiming for handsets, auto+IoT and data center to each contribute roughly a third of revenue.

Why it matters: it's Qualcomm's clearest attempt to diversify beyond a saturating premium-handset market into the AI-infrastructure build-out.
Qualcomm angle Tailwind
A credible third leg reduces smartphone cyclicality and gives Qualcomm a seat at the hyperscaler table; repurposing DC IP back into mobile/PC SoCs could lift the premium tier. Execution and the durability of hyperscaler capex are the live risks.
Investor angle Mixed
Bullish for QCOM specifically if DC traction is confirmed on the call; competitively a headwind for incumbent server-CPU and accelerator names as another entrant crowds in. Guidance is aggressive and not yet derisked. Not investment advice.
