China Watch

Alibaba ships Qwen3.8-Max to general availability — 2.4T parameters, benchmarks near Anthropic

SiliconANGLE / Bloomberg · Aug 3 ChinaFollow-up

Follow-up — what changed: Qwen3.8-Max was only previewed in the Aug 2 edition; it is now broadly available with published benchmarks, pricing and open weights due within a week.

Alibaba's Qwen team made Qwen3.8-Max broadly available on August 3 — a sparse mixture-of-experts model with 2.4T total parameters but only ~95B active per request, a 1M-token context, and multimodal (text/image/video) input. It ranked 2nd in Vision Arena and top-five in Text and Frontend-Code Arenas, with Alibaba claiming benchmark parity with Anthropic's flagship. Shares rallied on the news, and open weights are slated to follow within a week.

Why it matters: Frontier-class capability out of China at a fraction of Western cost keeps compressing the moat US labs are charging premiums to defend.

Second-order, but favorable: MoE designs with small active-parameter counts are exactly what fits on-device and edge NPUs. A steady supply of cheap, capable open-weight models strengthens Qualcomm's core structural bet that inference migrates toward the device. The benefit is downstream, not a direct catalyst.

Policy & Regulation

Washington takes equity in 7 chip firms for $874M — critics call it 'state capitalism'

Washington Post / NIST · Aug 3

Why it leads: the government-equity model is a first-order change to how the US compute supply chain gets funded — and this week is when it drew real political fire.

The Commerce Department's plan to hand $874M in CHIPS incentives to seven compute and AI companies in exchange for minority, non-controlling equity stakes drew sharp scrutiny this week, including a Washington Post op-ed branding it state capitalism. GlobalFoundries could receive up to $300M and Kepler up to $245M; the equity-for-funding model now spans 18 companies and up to $3.8B in awards since late 2025. Backers frame it as boosting taxpayer return and supply-chain security; critics warn it "could weaken market discipline."

Why it matters: The US government taking equity for chip subsidies reshapes both the financing and the governance of the compute supply chain.

Qualcomm angleWatch
Qualcomm isn't a recipient here, but its domestic-foundry options (GlobalFoundries, Intel 18A) are being reshaped by state money. Over time this could strengthen the US supply base it can diversify into, away from TSMC — while setting a precedent that government equity could eventually reach larger players. Speculative, worth tracking.
Investor angleMixed
An ownership and political-influence overhang for recipients, against a broader signal of deeper US industrial policy in compute. Supports domestic-capacity names but introduces a political risk premium. Not investment advice.

Semis & Hardware

Memory crunch deepens: DRAM up ~700% YoY, HBM 'sold out,' plus a fresh antitrust suit

Tom's Hardware / TradingKey · Aug 3 Follow-up

Follow-up — what changed: prior editions had the sold-out-HBM and memory-makers'-cash story; new this round are the ~700% YoY spot figure, explicit +10–20%/month guidance, and a US antitrust class action over the HBM transition.

Samsung and SK Hynix now warn AI-driven memory shortages could persist past 2027, with SK Hynix's HBM, DRAM and NAND capacity "essentially sold out" for 2026. DRAM spot prices are up roughly 700% year-on-year, and suppliers have told customers to plan for a further 10–20% increase per month through year-end; Micron has exited consumer memory entirely to prioritize AI customers. Newly, Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron face a US antitrust class action alleging the HBM transition is being used to inflate DRAM prices.

Why it matters: Memory is now the fastest-inflating line item in the bill of materials for every AI phone, PC and server.

Qualcomm angleHeadwind
Direct cost pressure. Rising LPDDR/DRAM prices squeeze Snapdragon-based handset and PC BOMs — the same dynamic Qualcomm already flagged in its own guidance on price hikes — pinching premium-tier margins and risking unit-demand softness. A structural headwind while the shortage runs.
Investor angleMixed
Bullish for memory makers (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron) on extraordinary pricing power; bearish for device OEMs and margin-sensitive hardware. The new antitrust suit is a tail risk to the bull case. Not investment advice.
