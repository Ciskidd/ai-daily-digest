01 — The Qualcomm story of the day, straight from the tape

Qualcomm's Q3 beats on revenue but guides light, blaming the memory crunch — and will raise prices Sept. 1

CNBC · Alphastreet · TIKR • Jul 29, after close
EarningsSemis

Qualcomm posted fiscal-Q3 revenue of $9.95B (up ~3% over the Street, high end of its guide) but adjusted EPS of $2.21 slightly missed and fell ~20% year-over-year. Handset revenue dropped 20% to $5.09B while combined automotive and IoT jumped 28%; management pinned the softness and light current-quarter guide ($9.7–10.5B) on the memory-driven cost crunch. CEO Cristiano Amon said Qualcomm will raise chip prices across the board starting September 1 and reaffirmed a $5B data-center revenue target for next year and $40B non-handset revenue by FY2029.

Why it matters · The memory shortage has now moved from a hardware-press story into a Tier-1 chipmaker's guidance and pricing — a real margin event, not a forecast.
Qualcomm angle Headwind
This is Qualcomm itself: a 20% handset decline plus memory-driven margin pressure and light guidance is a clear near-term headwind. The Sept. 1 price hikes defend margin but risk demand elasticity with cost-sensitive Chinese OEMs. Offsetting tailwind: auto/IoT +28% and the reiterated $5B data-center ramp keep the diversification thesis alive.
Investor angle Mixed
Revenue beat but the EPS miss and soft guide confirm the memory tax is hitting the fabless model, not just handset OEMs. The data-center narrative is the swing factor for the multiple; until $5B is on the board it stays a "show-me." Down ~5% YTD into the print, so some bad news was priced. Not investment advice.

02 — The capex verdict split the market in two

Microsoft pops 8% for holding its capex line; Meta sells off as spending climbs and margins fall

CNBC · Bloomberg · Money Morning • Jul 29, after close
EarningsHyperscalers

Microsoft rose ~8% after hours on FQ4 revenue of $90B (+18%), Azure growth of 43% in constant currency, and — critically — a capex envelope it declined to blow out. Investors, fresh off punishing Alphabet's first negative free cash flow last week, treated "still spending but not accelerating" as a relief. Meta, by contrast, grew revenue 28% to $60.8B but saw net income fall 14%, operating margin compress to 31% from 43%, and raised the low end of 2026 capex to $130B — and the stock extended its slide.

Why it matters · The market has flipped from rewarding AI spend to rewarding spending discipline; the gap between Microsoft and Meta last night is the whole thesis in one evening.
Qualcomm angle Neutral / Watch
Second-order. Sustained hyperscaler capex underpins cloud-inference demand, but Qualcomm's edge-inference bet arguably benefits more if the market starts forcing labs toward cheaper on-device offload. Watch whether "capex discipline" pressure accelerates interest in cost-efficient edge silicon.
Investor angle Bullish (MSFT) / Bearish (META)
Azure at 43% is the cleanest monetization print of the cycle and de-risks the AI-demand debate near-term. Meta shows the other side: capex up, margins down, monetization not yet visible. Rotation within the Mag 7 toward proven cloud ROI looks like the trade. Not investment advice.

03 — The memory supercycle's paradox, on full display

SK Hynix profit surges 557% to a record — and the stock falls ~10% as HBM4 shipments slip

Qz · Investing.com · TradingKey • Jul 29
EarningsMemory / HBM

SK Hynix reported record Q2 operating profit up 557% year-over-year and revenue up 257%, with an eye-watering 76.3% operating margin — yet shares dropped ~9.6% because both figures came in below elevated expectations. Analysts blamed HBM4 shipments landing lighter than hoped, pushing some revenue into later quarters. The company said HBM4 mass shipments began in Q2 and will ramp in the second half, and that first-half revenue crossed ₩100tn for the first time.

Why it matters · Even a 557% profit jump can't clear the bar now — expectations for AI memory are so high that the read-through is about the shortage and pricing, which flow straight into everyone else's cost base.
Qualcomm angle Headwind
Direct cost-input read-through: HBM/DRAM tightness and rising prices are exactly what Amon cited hours earlier for Qualcomm's light guide. Memory makers shifting 80%+ of advanced capacity to HBM keeps commodity DRAM scarce, pressuring Qualcomm's handset and IoT BOM through 2027.
Investor angle Mixed
The supercycle is intact — record margins prove pricing power — but "record profit, stock down" signals a lot is priced in and the bar is now shipment timing, not demand. HBM4 ramp cadence is the key H2 watch item for Hynix, Micron and the AI-memory complex. Not investment advice.

Policy & Regulation

More than 1,200 AI workers — including Amodei and rival labs' chief scientists — ask Washington to plan a slowdown

Fortune • Jul 29
PolicySafety

A statement titled "Pacing the Frontier" drew signatures from over 1,200 AI staff, including Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and cofounders, OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki, Meta chief scientist Shengjia Zhao and Google DeepMind safety head Anca Dragan. The unusual cross-company coalition — normally fierce competitors — calls for the government to help design a coordinated framework for pacing frontier-model deployment. It is a statement of intent rather than binding policy, but the roster gives it weight.

Why it matters · When the leadership of the four biggest labs jointly asks for guardrails, it raises the odds of federal action that could reshape the deployment (and capex) cadence the whole sector is priced on.
Qualcomm angle Watch
Speculative and second-order. Any frontier-model pacing regime lands on the largest labs first; edge/on-device inference (Qualcomm's turf) is largely out of scope and could even benefit if cloud frontier deployment is throttled. Worth watching only if controls broaden to hardware.
Investor angle Noise
No binding mechanism yet, so near-term price impact is limited. But it signals rising regulatory tail-risk to the aggressive-deployment narrative underpinning frontier-lab valuations; a real framework would be genuinely new information. Not investment advice.

Tencent's Hunyuan team open-sources AngelSpec, a full speculative-decoding stack

译 Translated · 腾讯混元 via 36氪 / DAMO • Jul 29
腾讯混元团队正式开源 AngelSpec：覆盖 drafter 训练、架构设计到线上部署的投机解码框架
ChinaCN ExclusiveOpen Source

Tencent's Hunyuan team released AngelSpec as open source — a speculative-decoding framework spanning drafter-model training, architecture design and production deployment. Speculative decoding speeds up LLM inference by having a small "drafter" model propose tokens that the large model verifies in batches, cutting latency and cost. Packaging the whole pipeline end-to-end lowers the bar for teams to deploy the technique in production. Not yet covered in English-language press.

Why it matters · Inference-efficiency tooling is where a lot of the real cost curve is being bent right now; open-sourcing a deployment-ready stack accelerates cheaper inference across the ecosystem.
Qualcomm angle Tailwind
Speculative decoding disproportionately helps constrained hardware — exactly the on-device/edge inference regime Qualcomm is betting on. Cheaper, faster local inference strengthens the case for running capable models on Snapdragon rather than round-tripping to the cloud. Second-order but directionally aligned with Qualcomm's core structural bet.
Investor angle Noise
No listed-name mover, but part of a steady drumbeat of inference-cost-reduction advances that, in aggregate, pressure the "compute demand is unbounded" thesis and favor efficiency winners over raw-FLOPs suppliers over time. Not investment advice.
